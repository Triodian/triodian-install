# Triodian and ISO/IEC 42001: our own position

> **Draft for Damian's decision (13 November 2026).** Nothing here is published until the dates and the choice
> below are confirmed. Once confirmed, the statement in section 2 goes on `/licensing` (or `/about`) and replaces
> the *Draft* row in the [security answer pack](security-answers.md), section 11.

## 1. What a client is asking

A risk or procurement lead who sees ISO/IEC 42001:2023 in our apps will ask whether Triodian, as a supplier,
runs its own AI management system to the standard, and whether it is certified. Today the honest answer is: not
yet, and no date has been set. A dated position answers the question even before certification.

Two facts bear on the dates:

- Certification bodies accredited for ISO/IEC 42001 already exist in Australia. JAS-ANZ runs an AI Management
  System Scheme, and has accredited at least four certification bodies under it (the first in 2025). The earlier
  note in the review, "once a certifier accredited under 42006 is available in Australia", is out of date: one is
  available now.
- ISO/IEC 42006:2025 is the standard accreditation bodies use to assess those certifiers. It is not something
  Triodian is certified against.

## 2. Draft statement (option A, recommended)

> **Triodian and ISO/IEC 42001.** Our apps help clients record AI governance in the terms of ISO/IEC 42001:2023,
> and we hold ourselves to the same standard. From **[1 April 2027]** we run Triodian's own AI management system to
> ISO/IEC 42001:2023, with its scope, AI policy, risk and impact assessment, Statement of Applicability, internal
> audit and management review in place. We intend to seek certification from a JAS-ANZ-accredited certification
> body, with the stage 1 audit planned for **[the second half of 2027]**. We are not certified today. This
> statement is dated **[date of approval]** and we will update it at each step.

Scope to name when the statement is approved: the design, development and operation of the Triodian app suite
and Triodian Start, and the advisory engagements run with them. The hardware tier would sit outside the first
scope.

## 3. The other options

| Option | What we would say | What it costs | What it leaves open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Run to 42001 and seek certification** (recommended) | Section 2 | Our own AIMS documents (most exist in draft in the apps' own outputs); an internal audit and a management review; certification fees for stage 1 and stage 2 and yearly surveillance | Nothing a client is likely to ask next |
| B. Run to 42001 without certification | "We run our AI management system to ISO/IEC 42001:2023 from [date]; we do not intend to seek certification at this stage" | The same internal work, no certification fees | A regulated client's supplier questionnaire may still mark the answer as a gap |
| C. No position yet | "Triodian is not certified to ISO/IEC 42001 and has not set a date" | Nothing now | The question comes back in every procurement |
